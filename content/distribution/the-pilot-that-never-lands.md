## LinkedIn post (English, 120-200 words)

MIT looked at 300+ organisations using generative AI. The finding: 95% got zero measurable return.

Not because the models failed. The demos impressed. The pilots died between the proof of concept and Monday morning.

After almost two decades inside large organisations, I can tell you exactly where they die: the handover. The people who build it know the problem from a briefing. The partners who own it move on after kick-off. The people who have to use it every day never saw either.

So the pilot runs on tidy sample data, and production runs on reality: duplicate records, a spreadsheet next to the official system, a colleague who has done the process well for twenty years.

The 5% who get through do something simple. They run in production from day one, they are used by the people who do the work, and they replace a real manual task.

The technology is rarely the problem. The design is.
