# The Outlier vs the strategy houses: McKinsey, BCG and Bain on AI

McKinsey, BCG and Bain are often called the MBB firms. For decades they have been the first call for chief executives facing a defining decision. Today each of them has a dedicated AI capability, and each of them publishes some of the most widely read research on how companies adopt AI.

If you lead a boutique firm of 50 to 100 people, you may wonder whether you need that level of firepower to get AI right. This article explains how the strategy houses approach AI, what they do exceptionally well, and why a partnership of your size is often better served by The Outlier.

We will be fair. The strategy houses are excellent. They are simply designed for a different client. In the language of the old Mac and PC adverts: they are the powerful machine in the corner office of a global corporation. We are the Mac on the desk of the partner who actually has to make it work on Monday.

## What the strategy houses bring

### McKinsey and QuantumBlack

McKinsey's AI arm is QuantumBlack. It was founded in London in 2009, acquired by McKinsey in 2015, and had grown to more than 1,000 practitioners by 2022 ([Consultancy.uk](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/31141/quantumblack-mckinseys-1000-strong-ai-consulting-division)). QuantumBlack combines data scientists, engineers and designers with McKinsey's industry consultants ([McKinsey, QuantumBlack](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/how-we-help-clients)). Its annual State of AI survey is one of the most cited sources on enterprise AI adoption ([McKinsey, The state of AI](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai)).

### BCG and BCG X

BCG launched BCG X in December 2022 as a tech build and design unit, bringing together nearly 3,000 technologists, scientists, engineers and designers ([Consultancy.com.au](https://www.consultancy.com.au/news/6229/bcg-launches-3000-strong-tech-build-design-unit-bcg-x)). In April 2026, BCG reported revenue of US$14.4 billion for 2025, a global headcount of about 33,500, and said that AI and technology work made up more than 40% of its revenue ([BCG press release](https://www.bcg.com/press/23april2026-bcg-revenue-22nd-consecutive-year-growth)).

### Bain and its OpenAI alliance

Bain announced a global services alliance with OpenAI in February 2023, combining its strategy and implementation work with OpenAI's tools ([Bain press release](https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/2023/bain--company-announces-services-alliance-with-openai-to-help-enterprise-clients-identify-and-realize-the-full-potential-and-maximum-value-of-ai/)). The firm describes itself as working across 67 cities in 40 countries ([Bain, About](https://www.bain.com/about/)).

These are real strengths. The strategy houses attract exceptional talent, they see patterns across hundreds of large clients, and they can stand up a team of specialists almost anywhere in the world. When a global retailer needs to rethink its whole operating model around AI, few organisations are better equipped.

## Why size changes the experience

The strategy houses are built around a model that has served them well for a long time. A small group of senior partners frames the problem and owns the client relationship. Teams of consultants, often early in their careers, do much of the analysis. Harvard Business Review described this classic pyramid in 2025 and argued that AI is pushing consulting toward leaner, more senior-heavy teams ([HBR](https://hbr.org/2025/09/ai-is-changing-the-structure-of-consulting-firms)). That transition is underway, but it reshapes large firms slowly.

For a boutique partnership, the classic model tends to create friction in a few places. These are general patterns across the industry and our own view, not statements about any specific engagement:

- **Strategy first, systems later.** The core product of a strategy house is a recommendation. Implementation may be handed to a separate unit, a partner firm or your own team.
- **Benchmarks from a different world.** Insight drawn from Fortune 500 clients is valuable, but a 70-person advisory firm does not run like a global bank.
- **Engagements sized for large budgets.** A firm that employs tens of thousands of people naturally focuses on work that justifies a full team.
- **The partner you meet in the pitch** is usually steering several engagements at once.

None of this is a flaw. It is what makes those firms work at their scale. It just does not match what a partnership of 50 to 100 people needs.

## When a strategy house is the right choice

Honesty first. A strategy house is the right call if you face a question that goes far beyond AI: a merger, a market exit, a full redesign of a multinational operating model. It is the right call if your board needs the comparative data that only comes from studying hundreds of large companies. It is the right call if you are building a proprietary AI product that needs dozens of data scientists for a year. And it is often the right call if a private equity owner already works with one of these firms and wants continuity.

In those cases, their scale is exactly what you are paying for.
