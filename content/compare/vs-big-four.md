# The Outlier vs the Big Four: AI transformation for a 50 to 100 person firm

Deloitte, PwC, EY and KPMG are remarkable organisations. They combine audit, tax, legal and consulting under one roof, and each of them has invested heavily in artificial intelligence. If you run a boutique advisory, mobility, HR, legal or recruitment firm, you have probably worked with one of them. Maybe one of them audits you.

So why would a firm of 60 people choose The Outlier for its AI work instead?

This article is our honest answer. It is not a takedown. The Big Four are very good at what they are built for. They are just built for something else. Think of the old Mac and PC adverts. The PC was not a bad machine. It was a capable, serious machine designed for the enterprise. The Mac was designed for a different kind of user. That is the difference we want to explain.

## What the Big Four do well

Start with the facts, because they are impressive.

Deloitte reported aggregate global revenue of US$70.5 billion for its 2025 fiscal year, with a workforce of more than 470,000 people across more than 150 countries and territories. The same announcement states that Deloitte has allocated over US$3 billion of investment in generative AI through fiscal year 2030 ([Deloitte, FY2025 revenue announcement](https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/about/press-room/global-revenue-announcement-2025.html)).

PwC US announced in April 2023 that it would invest US$1 billion over three years to expand its AI capabilities, in a relationship with Microsoft built on Azure OpenAI Service ([PwC US press release](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/about-us/newsroom/press-releases/pwc-us-makes-billion-investment-in-ai-capabilities.html)).

EY launched its EY.ai platform in September 2023, following US$1.4 billion of investment, and released its own large language model, EY.ai EYQ ([EY newsroom](https://www.ey.com/en_ch/newsroom/2023/09/ey-launches-artificial-intelligence-platform-ey-ai)).

KPMG committed US$2 billion to Microsoft cloud and AI services over five years in an expanded alliance announced in July 2023, with plans to train its global workforce on the new tools ([CFO Dive](https://www.cfodive.com/news/kpmg-to-spend-2b-in-expanded-ai-alliance-with-microsoft/687353/)).

These are serious commitments. The Big Four bring deep regulatory knowledge, global networks, audit-grade risk frameworks and benches of specialists in almost every field. When a multinational bank needs to roll out AI across forty countries under the eye of several regulators, that bench matters.

Our founder knows this world from the inside. Fariza Sbaa spent nearly two decades in HR, Global Mobility, Governance, Compliance and Transformation at ING Bank, Heineken International and Media.Monks. She has worked alongside large advisory firms on large programmes. She respects what they do.

## Where a boutique firm sits in that picture

Here is the structural reality. A firm with hundreds of thousands of people needs large engagements to keep that machine running. That is not a criticism. It is arithmetic.

The classic consulting model is often described as a pyramid: a small number of senior partners who sell and steer the work, supported by larger teams of junior staff who do much of the analysis. Harvard Business Review described this model in 2025 and noted that AI is pushing firms toward smaller, more senior-heavy teams ([HBR, "AI Is Changing the Structure of Consulting Firms"](https://hbr.org/2025/09/ai-is-changing-the-structure-of-consulting-firms)). The shift is real, but it takes time inside an organisation of that size.

For a 70-person partnership, this tends to show up in a few ways. These are general industry patterns and our opinion, not claims about any specific engagement:

- **The senior person who sells the work is not always the person who does it.** In a leveraged model, that is the point of the structure.
- **Your project competes for attention** with clients whose budgets are many times larger than yours.
- **Scoping, approvals and risk checks take time**, because a global network must protect its brand and its audit independence everywhere at once.
- **Methods are designed for scale.** A framework built for a 20,000-person company can feel heavy on a firm where the managing partner still reviews every proposal.

There is one more point worth knowing. If a Big Four firm audits your company, independence rules can limit what advisory work the same network may take on. For public-interest entities in the EU, those limits are written into law ([Regulation (EU) No 537/2014, Article 5](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A32014R0537)). Most firms of your size are not public-interest entities, but it is a fair question to ask before you start.
